# Space view

Zoom out past the local scene's maximum distance and keep scrolling (or pinching): the view fades through black
(about 0.7 s) to the Earth seen from space, with the orbits of the Sentinel satellites. Zoom back in past the closest
distance, press `Esc`, or use the "Back to station" button to return to the station at the camera state you left.
While in space view the local scene is not rendered; the dashboard, search and pass card keep working, and picking a
satellite brings you back to the station.

## How it works

| Piece | Module | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Hand-over state machine | `src/scene/space-transition.mjs` | Pure: `local -> to-space -> space -> to-local`. The scene switches at the midpoint of the fade, behind a fully dark overlay. Only a sustained push at the limit counts (about three wheel notches), so a normal zoom never triggers it. |
| Geometry | `src/scene/space-math.mjs` | Pure: lat/lon to xyz, ECI to scene frame, ring simplification, footprint ring, occlusion by the Earth. 1 unit = 1 km, Earth centre at the origin, +y north. |
| Earth | `src/scene/space-earth.mjs` | 64 x 48 sphere (6371 km), a 2048 x 1024 canvas texture painted at run time from the coastline data, 30 degree graticule, atmosphere rim, day/night terminator from the sun direction. No image files. If the canvas texture fails, a plain blue ball with the graticule is used. |
| Orbits | `src/scene/space-orbits.mjs`, `src/core/orbit-loop.mjs` | One `LineLoop` of 150 points per satellite: one orbital period in ECI propagated from the stored orbit data at the simulated time. Rebuilt only when the simulated clock moves more than 10 minutes (or the data changes). A bright marker per satellite follows the simulated clock. Colours by family as in the mini globe. |
| Sun | `src/core/sun-eci.mjs` | Low-precision solar formula (about 0.01 degrees), unit vector in ECI. |
| Orchestration | `src/scene/space-view.mjs` | Second `THREE.Scene` on the same renderer with its own camera and `OrbitControls` (left drag rotates, wheel/pinch zooms, 1.25 to 9 Earth radii). |

The Earth mesh is turned about the polar axis by GMST (`gstime` from the vendored satellite.js, reached only through
`src/core/sgp4.mjs`), so inertial orbits are drawn correctly against the rotating Earth. Orbits are drawn at their true
radius (about 7000 km), so they hug the Earth: that is the real geometry, not an artistic compression.

Labels: only the station, the selected satellite and the satellite under the pointer are named, and a label is hidden
where it would sit under the interface. The Maspalomas marker has a faint ring: the approximate visibility footprint of
the station's elevation mask at a typical 700 km altitude (same reference as the mini globe).

## Coastline data

`src/data/coastlines.mjs` is generated by `tools/build-coastlines.mjs` from **Natural Earth 110m land**
(`ne_110m_land.geojson`, https://www.naturalearthdata.com/, public domain: "Made with Natural Earth. Free vector and raster
map data"). The tool downloads the file once at development time (or reads a local copy), checks that every coordinate is
a finite number inside the lon/lat range (the download is treated as untrusted input), drops specks under about
1 square degree, simplifies the rings (Douglas-Peucker) and rounds to one decimal to stay under about 60 KB.
Regenerate with `node tools/build-coastlines.mjs [path-to-geojson]`. Nothing is fetched at run time.

## Limits

- Coastlines are 110m generalised: islands such as the Canaries are too small to appear. The station marker shows the position.
- Orbit loops are one period from the current simulated time in an inertial frame; they do not model drag, manoeuvres
  or orbit-data age (see the freshness indicators in the dashboard).
- The sun direction and the Earth orientation are low-precision models, good for day/night and orientation, not for ephemerides.
- No CelesTrak request is made by the space view; it only uses the orbit data already loaded.
